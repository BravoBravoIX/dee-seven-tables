# v1.0.1 — John Dee's Seven Tables of Creation: the lost cells cannot be placed (a test of 20 layout rules)

Working paper (not peer reviewed), with its code, data and results. The paper is `paper/dee-seven-tables.pdf` in this archive.

**What changed.** Documentation update: adds a "How AI was used" section, tidies wording and removes files not needed to check the results. Results and data unchanged. Run-record hashes refreshed; numbers unchanged.

**Abstract.** On 29 April 1582 John Dee drew seven 7 × 7 tables, joined as a cross, from which the names of 49 angels are read (British Library, Sloane MS 3188 ff. 37v–38r). The leaf is broken along its fold, and cells of tables 2 and 3 are lost. Every name is known, so the number and letter of each lost entry are known; what is lost is which cell each stood in. Whitby (1982) wrote that the positions "cannot be ascertained" because "there is no rule for individual placings". This note tests that statement. From a cell-by-cell transcription of Whitby's drawing (two reads by Claude, which differ at 2 of 343 cells; a machine witness agrees on 337 of 343 entries), the lost entries are 13R, 30R, 36N and 41A in table 2 and ten entries in table 3; the surviving fragments allow 6 and 1,080 placements. Twenty families of layout rules, fixed in advance, were scored on 720 legible cells hidden from them, against 20,000 copies of the tables with their numbers shuffled. No family passes after a Holm correction (best: p = 0.013, Holm 0.25), so by the pre-registered stop rule no lost cell is placed. Two patterns seen earlier (consecutive numbers a knight's move apart in table 2, and king-adjacent in table 4) survive the checked transcription but not an allowance for how they were found, and remain leads only. The evidence does not decide where the lost entries stood, which agrees with Whitby.

**Results in brief**
- The drawing reads cell by cell: 343 cells; two reads by Claude differ at 2 cells, both settled at higher zoom; a machine witness (Claude API) agrees on 337 of 343 entries.
- Lost entries: table 2, 13R, 30R, 36N, 41A; table 3, 4S, 5U, 6A, 13I, 19A, 28I, 37A, 38R, 44G, 48R.
- The fragments allow 6 placements in table 2 and 1,080 in table 3 (exhaustive enumeration).
- No layout rule predicts hidden cells better than chance: best family p = 0.013 before correction, 0.25 after (Holm, 20 families; 20,000 shuffled controls). So the lost cells cannot be placed, as Whitby (1982) wrote.
- Two patterns are kept as leads only: knight's moves in table 2, king's moves in table 4.
